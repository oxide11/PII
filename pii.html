The Science of Re-identification

Why does combining seemingly safe data (like a ZIP code and Gender) trigger a strict PI violation? The answer lies in Linkage Attacks and the mathematical concept of k-anonymity.

The Linkage Attack Mechanism

Data is rarely breached in a vacuum. Hackers re-identify "anonymous" tables by overlaying them with public datasets.

The 87% Rule (The Mosaic Effect)

You might think thousands of people share your ZIP code and birthday. Mathematically, they do not. Privacy scientists proved that the combination of just three demographic traits acts as a unique fingerprint for the vast majority of the population.

Academic Citation:
"It was found that 87% (216 million of 248 million) of the population in the United States had reported characteristics that likely made them unique based only on [5-digit ZIP, gender, date of birth]."

— Sweeney, L. (2000). Simple Demographics Often Identify People Uniquely. Carnegie Mellon University, Data Privacy Working Paper 3.
